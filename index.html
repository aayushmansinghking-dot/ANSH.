Father of the Nation

Mahatma Gandhi was not just a leader, but a living conscience of humanity. He proved that truth and non-violence are stronger than empires. His life was a message, and his message became a movement.

Early Life

Mohandas Karamchand Gandhi was born on 2 October 1869 in Porbandar, Gujarat. Raised in a deeply spiritual environment, Gandhi learned values of honesty, discipline, and compassion from his mother Putlibai. He married Kasturba Gandhi at a young age and later studied law in London.

Freedom Struggle

Gandhi’s struggle began in South Africa, where racial injustice awakened his mission. Returning to India, he led the masses through non-violent resistance. Movements like Non-Cooperation, Salt March, and Quit India reshaped history. India finally gained independence on 15 August 1947.

Historic Timeline

1869: Birth in Porbandar
1893: South Africa journey
1915: Return to India
1930: Dandi March
1942: Quit India Movement
1947: Independence
1948: Martyrdom

Words That Changed the World

“Be the change that you wish to see in the world.”

“Truth is God.”

“An eye for an eye makes the whole world blind.”

Gandhian Philosophy

Gandhi believed in Ahimsa, Satya, self-reliance, and unity. His philosophy teaches humanity that peace is not weakness, but the highest form of strength.
